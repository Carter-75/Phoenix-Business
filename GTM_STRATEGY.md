# Go-to-market strategy
Status: initial hypothesis. Owner: Carter. No offer or channel is validated.

## Confirmed boundaries
Serve clients throughout the USA. Maximum stated capacity: 10 projects a month. Minimum project price: $500. Setup cost must be below $100. Total monthly marketing cap: $400, including existing ads, software, and contractors. These limits do not authorize spending or publication.

## Initial customer profile
Owner-led residential painting companies with an existing business, visible work, and a specific website problem. Start manual research in Wisconsin, then expand across US markets if replies and margins support it. Geography narrows the experiment, not the service area. Prefer an owner who can approve a project, supplies photos and service details, and can answer new inquiries.
- Painting: visual work fits design demonstrations; estimate requests fit a simple form; owner contacts can be public. Seasonal demand is a risk. Project values and buying budgets must be confirmed on calls.
- HVAC/plumbing: urgent calls can be valuable, but emergency routing and stronger agency competition add complexity. No verified local acquisition cost yet.
- Restaurants: portfolio demos fit well, but menus, ordering, and frequent updates can raise support cost. No evidence of buyer budgets.
- Medical/dental: potential budgets do not justify the added privacy and integration burden for a first low-cost test.
These are qualitative judgments. Local search results prove active businesses, not market demand for Phoenix. Do not fabricate keyword volume or close rates.

## Competitor evidence
[Petz Web Design](https://petzwebdesign.com/) offers small-business static websites and responsive design in Madison. These features alone do not differentiate Phoenix. [Winnebago Web Designs](https://winnebagowebdesigns.com/) targets small businesses nationally; the search result confirmed the positioning, but direct pricing retrieval failed. Do not quote an unverified competitor price.
The defensible initial distinction is direct founder access, narrow scope, clear ownership, and a tested inquiry path. AI use supports delivery; it is not the customer outcome.

## Proposed offers — require approval before sale
Headline: “Make it easier for customers to request a painting estimate.”
Entry offer: free review of one public page with three specific fixes. Spend no more than 20 minutes on each review. No growth guarantee.
$500 Quick Fix: one existing page, one contact action, mobile cleanup, one revision round. Target at most 4 build hours. Excludes rebuilds, booking systems, copywriting from scratch, e-commerce, and ongoing support.
$1,500 Lead Website: up to five pages, supplied brand assets and photos, service copy editing, contact form, mobile checks, basic metadata, and handover. Two consolidated revision rounds. Estimate two weeks after all inputs and written scope approval. Time target: 12 build hours, then confirm using actual jobs.
Custom systems: quote separately. Do not sell custom application scope for $500.
Care: propose $49/month for limited monitoring and maintenance, with client-paid hosting and domain. No included content edits. Do not sell this until a monthly labor allowance and provider costs are measured. Preserve existing client plans.
Propose a 50% deposit and 50% after preview acceptance, before launch. Define acceptance and delay handling in writing. Refund rules, IP transfer, third-party licenses, and liability need qualified review. Do not use a vague satisfaction guarantee.
Ownership proposal: client owns paid custom deliverables after final payment; third-party software keeps its own license. Use client-owned domain and hosting accounts. Fix defects against agreed scope for 14 days after launch. New requests need a quote. This is proposed wording, not a legal opinion or a change to current contracts.

## Economics
[Stripe standard domestic-card pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing) lists 2.9% + $0.30 per successful transaction. Actual account fees, Billing fees, taxes, refunds, and international cards may differ.
One $500 charge leaves $485.20 before labor, hosting, marketing, taxes, and support. Two $250 charges leave $484.90. At $100 acquisition cost and four hours valued at an assumed $30/hour, contribution is $264.90 before other costs. At 10 hours it falls to $84.90. Revenue is not profit.
Two $750 charges leave $1,455.90. At 12 hours valued at $30/hour and $150 acquisition cost, contribution is $945.90 before other costs. All labor values and acquisition costs here are scenarios, not results.
Set an initial acquisition ceiling of $100 for Quick Fix and $150 for Lead Website. At an assumed 20% qualified-call close rate, the corresponding paid qualified-call ceiling is $20 or $30. Recalculate with actual close rates. A $3 click and 5% click-to-qualified-call rate would cost $60 per call: too high for these targets. These are sensitivity examples, not CPC forecasts.

## 90-day plan
Weeks 1–2: restore tracking and lead capture; inspect 20 businesses per week; prepare 10 personalized messages per week; record one useful video per week. Sending requires approval. Target process completion, not invented sales.
Weeks 3–4: after approval send at most five new messages per weekday, at most 50 initial contacts across the first test. One follow-up each. Aim to learn from five buyer conversations; this is a target, not a forecast. Review search terms twice weekly if Ads launches.
Weeks 5–8: retain one winning message and niche. Research 20 new firms weekly. Keep volume flat until quality gates pass. Seek two qualified calls per week; if there are none, revise problem selection and offer before adding spend. Deliver and time every project.
Weeks 9–13: seek three qualified calls per week and one paid project per week as operating targets. Expand to a second US region only after at least three paid projects and positive measured contribution. Maintain no more than two active builds until actual delivery data supports more. Never exceed the stated 10-project monthly maximum.
If replies are low, fix list relevance. If calls are unqualified, tighten the message and form. If proposals fail, review scope and proof. If margin fails, reduce scope or raise price. Do not solve every bottleneck with more traffic.

Lead with clear scope, useful pages, and direct work with Carter. Keep the AI assistant labeled; never claim AI-free development.
